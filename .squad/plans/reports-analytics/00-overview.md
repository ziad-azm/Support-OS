## Dependency notes

This feature maps to **EPIC 11 — Reports & Analytics** in `SupportOs backlog.MD` (lines 628-663). The epic's own header declares it depends on Ticket Management (EPIC 4, complete), SLA (EPIC 7, complete), and Portal CSAT (`PORTAL-5`, complete) — all three of the data sources its reports aggregate already exist and are populated.

`RPT-0` (story 55) is the 🔑 foundation every other story in the epic builds on: `RPT-1` (Ticket Reports), `RPT-2` (SLA Performance, also depends on `SLA-1`), `RPT-3` (Agent Performance), and `RPT-4` (Customer Satisfaction, also depends on `PORTAL-5`) each add one report on top of it, and `RPT-5` (Management Dashboards) depends on `RPT-1..4` and explicitly reuses `RPT-0`'s charts rather than adding a chart type (`SupportOs backlog.MD:663`).

**Its design input already exists and is not re-derived.** `CONVENTIONS.md` § 25's "Chart-type guidance (for `RPT-0`)" (lines 1619-1652) was written by Story 38 (`DSN-3`) specifically for this story — a seven-row table naming the chart type, library options, and color guidance for each `RPT-*` data shape, plus an accessibility floor (a data-table or text-summary fallback for every chart; color always paired with a label, line style, or shape; keyboard focus revealing what hover reveals). Story 55 implements against that table rather than choosing chart types itself. See [`../design-intelligence-ui-ux-system/00-overview.md`](../design-intelligence-ui-ux-system/00-overview.md).

**Story 55 ships infrastructure and no domain surface** — the same shape Story 27 (`SLA-0`) took for background jobs (see [`../sla-automation/27-story-background-jobs-foundation-SUPPORTOS-49.md`](../sla-automation/27-story-background-jobs-foundation-SUPPORTOS-49.md)). Four things a reader would expect it to add are deliberately deferred to `RPT-1`, each forced by an explicit project rule rather than by preference:

| Deferred to `RPT-1` | The rule that forces it |
|---|---|
| `Permissions.REPORTS_VIEW` + its grant migration | `apps/core/permissions.py:5-7` — *"a permission that no view declares must not be grantable"*, and lines 22-24, constants land *"in the same change as the viewset that declares them"*. |
| `apps/reports/urls.py` + the `config/api_urls.py` line | `backend/apps/README.md:52-56` — *"an app gets a `urls.py` when it has a route… an empty file is a promise the codebase has not made yet"*. |
| `features/reports/`, its route, sidebar link, and i18n namespace | Story 55 ships no screen; its frontend surface is `shared/ui/chart/` plus a `chart` block in the existing `common` namespace. |
| Bullet/Gauge and Waffle charts | § 25 assigns them to `RPT-2`/`RPT-5` and `RPT-4`; each has exactly one first consumer and needs chart-specific colors § 25 says are *"not `--chart-1..5` slots"*. They are built **inside** `ChartFrame` by those stories — which is what makes `ChartFrame` the reusable wrapper the intake asked for. |

Line and Bar charts are the exception and **do** ship in story 55, for a structural reason rather than a stylistic one: `no-restricted-imports` (`frontend/.oxlintrc.json:8-18`) forbids one feature importing another's code, so a Line chart built inside `RPT-1` could never be reused by `RPT-2` or `RPT-4`. `shared/ui/` is the only place they can live, and `RPT-0` is the only story that can put them there.

**Three findings from story 55 that later stories inherit rather than rediscover:**

- **The export query parameter is `?export=csv`, never `?format=csv`.** DRF's `DefaultContentNegotiation` reads `?format=` as a renderer override and raises `Http404` when no renderer matches (`rest_framework/negotiation.py:41-45, 80-89`); this project registers exactly one renderer, format `"json"`. Worse, `APIView.initial()` negotiates *before* it checks permissions (`rest_framework/views.py:404-420`), so `?format=csv` would 404 before any view body or permission check ran, with no way for a subclass to intervene. `BaseReportView` names the parameter `export` and records why in a comment.
- **A CSV response is a plain `HttpResponse`, not a DRF `Response`** — that is what bypasses `EnvelopeJSONRenderer`. Verified against `finalize_response`, which attaches a renderer only to a DRF `Response` (`rest_framework/views.py:434`), and already relied on once by `AttachmentViewSet.download` (Story 21). No `CsvRenderer` is added alongside `PlainTextRenderer`.
- **No chart library, decided once for the whole epic.** Two of the four chart types EPIC 11 needs (Bullet, Waffle) have no mainstream React library implementation, and § 25 lists only D3/Plotly/Custom SVG/Custom CSS Grid for them — so no single library covers the epic, and adopting one would mean two rendering paradigms plus custom work anyway to satisfy § 25's accessibility floor and this project's RTL discipline. Recharts was the alternative considered and rejected. Same call Story 51 made against the shadcn `sidebar` component and Story 18 made against `django-filter`.

**Gap-filling is the non-obvious core of the aggregation half.** A trend series with a missing bucket draws a straight line across a real zero and silently lies; `bucketed_counts` therefore returns every bucket in range, `value: 0` included. Two related quiet-failure modes are pinned down in the plan's own edge cases and verification: Django's `TruncWeek` truncates to **Monday** (a spine that steps in 7-day increments from an arbitrary start matches nothing and renders the whole series as zeros), and a default `Meta.ordering` — `Ticket`'s is `("-created_at",)` — joins itself into the `GROUP BY` and fragments an aggregate into one row per record unless `.order_by()` is called explicitly.

**Story 56 (`RPT-1`, Ticket Reports) is the foundation's first consumer, and picks up all five deferred items above** — `reports.view` plus its grant migration, `apps/reports/urls.py`, the `config/api_urls.py` include, and the whole `features/reports/` folder. It adds two `BaseReportView` subclasses rather than one endpoint: `GET /api/reports/tickets/volume/` (trend, `?series=`) and `GET /api/reports/tickets/breakdown/` (categories, `?dimension=`). Two, because `BaseReportView.get_report` returns a flat list of dicts so the CSV and the JSON are the same rows — a single endpoint returning both a trend and a breakdown could not export either cleanly. The split also maps 1:1 onto § 25's two `RPT-1` rows (Line for the trend, Bar for the categories).

**Three findings from story 56 that `RPT-2`…`RPT-5` inherit:**

- **A ticket has no `channel` field — "by channel" means its earliest INBOUND message's channel**, derived by a `Subquery` annotation in `apps/reports/tickets.py::with_origin_channel`, never persisted. Verified against this project's Postgres: the alias is a valid `.values()` key and a valid `exclude(...__isnull=True)` target, so `grouped_counts` consumes it unchanged. Tickets created directly in the staff UI have no inbound message and count under a "Created directly" label rather than being dropped — 6 of 11 rows in the current database, so dropping them would understate volume by more than half.
- **Story 56 fixes a real crash in story 55's `bucketed_counts`.** Its series-key sort (`aggregation.py:153`) was a bare `sorted({...})`, which raises `TypeError: '<' not supported between instances of 'str' and 'NoneType'` the moment `series_field` yields a SQL NULL — reproduced live with the origin-channel annotation. `grouped_counts` already had an `include_null`/`null_label` policy and `bucketed_counts` had none; story 56 gives it the same contract. **`RPT-3` would hit the identical crash** splitting by `assigned_agent` (nullable, `SET_NULL`).
- **A `YYYY-MM-DD` bucket string rendered through `useFormatters().date()` shows the WRONG DAY west of Greenwich.** `formatDate` does `new Date(value)`, and JS parses a bare date as UTC midnight — `'2026-01-01'` renders as "Dec 31, 2025" in `America/New_York`. Every bucket label passes `{ dateStyle: 'medium', timeZone: 'UTC' }`. This is invisible from a UTC or positive-offset machine, so it is checked by overriding the browser timezone, not by looking.

**Dimensions are a whitelist, not a field name off the query string.** `apps/reports/tickets.py::DIMENSION_FIELDS` maps four wire values to queryset fields and `parse_dimension` rejects everything else — `grouped_counts` interpolates its `field` argument straight into `.values()`, so an unvalidated `?dimension=` would be an arbitrary-column read for anyone holding `reports.view`.

**Story 57 (`RPT-2`, SLA Performance) is the batching strategy Story 28's own `## Prerequisites` explicitly deferred.** Story 28 (`SLA-1`) scoped SLA status to single-ticket reads only, naming the N+1 cost of `compute_sla_status` (two extra queries per ticket) as the reason, and said a future story could add list-level SLA reporting "once there is a batching strategy... worth its own design pass." Story 57 is that story: it does not call `compute_sla_status` in a loop. It re-derives the same classification against a bulk-annotated `Ticket` queryset — two `Subquery` annotations (the same technique `RPT-1`'s `with_origin_channel` established) plus one bulk `SLAPolicy`/`OrganizationSettings` fetch — bringing the cost from `2N+1` queries down to exactly 3, regardless of ticket count. Verified equal to the single-ticket path on a real ticket in this project's database before being trusted as the report's foundation. To guarantee the two paths can never silently disagree, `apps/sla/policy.py::_dimension_status` (the `met`/`breached`/`pending` classifier) was promoted from private to public and is now called by both.

**Its trend metric is an average, not a count, which breaks `RPT-0`'s own gap-fill rule on purpose.** `bucketed_counts`'s `value: 0` for an empty bucket is correct for a count (zero tickets is a real zero) but would be a lie for an average response/resolution time (implying instant service where there is simply no data). Story 57 therefore introduces the first documented exception to `CONVENTIONS.md` § 27 point 2: `apps/reports/sla.py::sla_trend` omits an empty bucket instead of gap-filling it, recorded as § 27's new point 10 for any future average-shaped report to follow.

**It is also the first real consumer of the Gauge/Bullet chart type `RPT-0` deliberately deferred** — and, per § 25's own text, not a single-consumer case: `RPT-5` (Management Dashboards) explicitly reuses the same chart unchanged for its own KPI grid. That two-consumer count is what puts the new `GaugeChart` in `shared/ui/chart/` rather than inside `features/reports/` — the identical `no-restricted-imports` reasoning that already placed `LineChart`/`BarChart` there. Its qualitative-zone colors (`#FFCDD2`/`#FFF9C4`/`#C8E6C9`) come directly from § 25's own literal spec, now finally given real values rather than left as "recorded... for `RPT-0` to name as its own tokens when it exists."

**Story 58 (`RPT-3`, Agent Performance) is the third and simplest bulk-computation report — one endpoint, three metrics, no new frontend chart.** `CONVENTIONS.md` § 25 row 5 assigns it the same Horizontal Bar Chart `RPT-1`'s breakdown already uses (`orientation="horizontal"`, unused by any prior story), so `shared/ui/chart/` gains nothing new. It extends `RPT-2`'s bulk-`Subquery`-annotation pattern to two annotations at once (a ticket's resolved-at time and its linked feedback rating), verified live to run in exactly 4 queries regardless of ticket or agent count. Its one endpoint takes a required `?metric=handled|resolution|csat`, the same required-whitelist shape `RPT-1`'s `?dimension=` established — not three separate endpoints, since all three metrics are one bulk computation over the same ticket population.

**Two findings from story 58 worth carrying forward:** an agent, unlike a ticket dimension, is arbitrary user data with no frontend-translatable enum — so this is the first `RPT-*` report whose rows carry a server-resolved `label` (via `User.get_full_name()`, the codebase's one established display-name convention) rather than a bare `key`/`value` pair the frontend translates itself. And CSAT is reported as a single "% satisfied" scalar (0-1, reusing `RPT-2`'s `rate` convention) rather than a weighted score across all three `Feedback.Rating` values — deliberately, since no numeric weighting for "neutral" exists anywhere in this codebase and `RPT-4` (story 59) owns the real satisfied/neutral/dissatisfied breakdown chart.

**Story 59 (`RPT-4`, Customer Satisfaction) is the first `RPT-*` report needing NO new backend domain module.** Unlike `RPT-1`/`RPT-2`/`RPT-3`, `Feedback.rating` needs no join, no bulk resolver, no annotation — `apps/reports/aggregation.py`'s existing `bucketed_counts`/`grouped_counts` work against `Feedback` exactly as they are, called directly from the view (the same "no bespoke helper needed" shape `TicketVolumeReportView`/`TicketBreakdownReportView` already used inline). It is also the first consumer of a genuinely new chart type since `RPT-2`'s `GaugeChart`: a Waffle chart for the satisfied/neutral/dissatisfied breakdown (§ 25 row 6, explicitly not Pie/Donut on accessibility grounds), built on a **largest-remainder ("Hamilton") percentage-to-square allocation** — verified algebraically across 8 cases to always sum to exactly 100 squares, where a naive per-category `Math.round()` can silently land on 99 or 101. `WaffleChart` goes into `shared/ui/chart/` on the strength of `CONVENTIONS.md` § 27 point 6's own unconditional wording, even though — unlike `GaugeChart` — no second consumer is currently named for it, extending that written rule consistently rather than reopening it as a fresh per-story judgment call. The story also promotes a `formatBucket` helper, independently duplicated across `RPT-1`'s and `RPT-2`'s own report pages, into a shared `features/reports/lib/` module — the third occurrence being what crosses this project's own "used by two or more → move it" threshold (`CONVENTIONS.md` § 8).

**Note on testing:** per standing project policy this project authors no automated tests. Story 55 adds none. Its checks are `manage.py check`/`makemigrations --check`/`ruff`, direct `manage.py shell` exercise of every helper against real seeded data (including a `sum == count` assertion that catches aggregate fragmentation and a Monday assertion that catches the week spine), an Arabic CSV opened in Excel to prove the UTF-8 BOM, the frontend's `lint`/`format:check`/`check:rtl`/`build`, an `en`/`ar` key-set diff, and a bilingual visual pass over the charts including a greyscale check that the line dash patterns — not the hues — carry the series distinction.
